# High-saturation art-market recipe

## Visual thesis

Vivid, playful, and curated. Make the deck feel like a creative-market poster system: energetic at first glance, orderly on closer reading.

## Signature

- Use a small set of high-saturation colors with one grounding dark or neutral tone.
- Mix oversized display type, cropped imagery, sticker-like labels, blocks, and unexpected scale shifts.
- Favor asymmetric balance and controlled overlap.
- Treat each slide as a poster in the same campaign rather than a corporate page with colorful decoration.
- Let whitespace become an active shape; do not fill every gap.

## Typography and rhythm

Use one expressive display behavior and one highly readable information behavior. Contrast large titles with compact metadata. Break the grid deliberately, then restore order through shared baselines, repeated crops, or color roles.

## Useful silhouettes

- Poster cover with oversized title and one dominant visual
- Vendor or artist lineup as a typographic field
- Program schedule with color-coded time bands
- Product or artwork collage with concise captions
- Map, wayfinding, or zone guide with bold landmarks
- Closing poster with call to action and event details

## Remix controls

- More fashion: use sharper cropping, black accents, and editorial type.
- More craft: use warm paper texture, hand-made marks, and irregular edges.
- More pop: increase primary colors and geometric shapes.
- More underground: use dark grounds, fluorescent accents, and compressed typography.

## Failure signals

Every element competes for attention; saturation has no hierarchy; overlaps reduce readability; poster language is applied to dense operational tables; decorative stickers feel random; the deck loses continuity from slide to slide.
